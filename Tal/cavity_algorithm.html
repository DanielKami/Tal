Cavity Detection in TAL

TAL implements a voxel-based cavity (void / pore / channel) detector that works for all 230 space groups, including triclinic and other non-orthogonal cells. The algorithm is fully integrated into the desktop application and produces quantitative geometric, topological and property-mapped results.

1. Definition

A cavity is any connected region of empty voxels that lies outside the van der Waals surface of every atom (including symmetry-generated atoms). Empty voxels are those for which the signed distance function (SDF) is positive.

The method therefore detects:

  • closed cavities (isolated voids),
  • surface-connected voids,
  • channels / tunnels that percolate through the unit cell,
  • interconnected 3-D pore networks.

2. Crystal-aligned voxel grid

The grid is built in fractional coordinates along the actual unit-cell vectors \( \mathbf{a} \), \( \mathbf{b} \), \( \mathbf{c} \): 

p = origin + fx·a + fy·b + fz·c

This guarantees that voxel faces align with the cell walls even for skewed cells. A Cartesian grid would introduce systematic errors in wall detection, periodic wrapping and volume for non-orthogonal systems.

Voxel volume

The volume of one voxel is the absolute value of the scalar triple product of the step vectors: 

stepA = Δfx · a
stepB = Δfy · b
stepC = Δfz · c
Vvoxel = | (stepA × stepB) · stepC |

(Implemented in CellParameters.GetVoxelVolume().) The older approximate formula that multiplies stepX·a · stepY·b · stepZ·c · sinα · sinβ · sinγ is not used; the triple-product form is exact for any cell angles.

3. Detection pipeline

  1. Atom generation — all symmetry equivalents inside the unit cell plus a margin (ScanMargin = 2 Å) of neighbouring cells are generated. Effective radius of atom i: 
    r_eff(i) = rvdW(i) · VdWMultiplier + ProbeRadius
    When thermal motion is enabled the effective shape becomes an ellipsoid whose half-axes are r_eff + √|λk| (1σ ADP amplitudes from the Un matrix).
  2. SDF computation — for every voxel the signed distance to the nearest atom surface is calculated. SDF < 0 inside an atom, SDF > 0 in free space. Distant voxels are truncated.
  3. Flood-fill — connected empty (SDF > 0) regions are extracted (CavityFillAndSpread). Regions smaller than MinCavityVolume (default 0.1 Å³) are discarded.
  4. Wall / topology classification — each cavity records which of the six unit-cell walls it touches. Topology type = number of touched walls (0–6).
  5. Channel detection — continuous paths that connect opposite walls are identified. Direction labels: XX, YY, ZZ, XXYY, XXZZ, YYZZ, XXYYZZ. Path geometry, length, width, helicity and cell crossings are computed by an A* pathfinder (ChannelPathFinder) operating on the semi-periodic grid.
  6. Marching Cubes — a triangle mesh is generated from the SDF isosurface of each cavity (smoothing level controllable). Surface area is the sum of triangle areas.
  7. Derived quantities — volume (= voxel count × Vvoxel), surface area, sphericity, normalised V/N and S/N (divided by Z), and error estimates.

5. Quantitative results (per cavity)

  • Volume and volume error (Å³)
  • Surface area and surface error (Å²)
  • Sphericity = \( (36\pi V^2)^{1/3} / S \) (1.0 = sphere)
  • V/N and S/N (normalised by formula units Z)
  • Channel directions and path metrics (length, min/avg/max width, helicity, crossings)
  • Topology type and list of touched walls

6. Surface property mapping

Atomic properties can be projected onto the cavity mesh vertices by spatial proximity (weighted average inside a configurable search radius):

  • Charge — partial charges from the SQE (Simple Quantum Electronegativity) method (default; Gasteiger/PEOE is also available in the codebase)
  • Hydrophobicity — Eisenberg–Weiss consensus scale
  • Atomic number
  • Van der Waals radius
  • Occupancy

Colour scales are adjustable; the mapping is independent of the detection step.

7. Multi-scale VdW analysis

The detector can be run across a range of VdW multipliers. Because the effective radius of every atom scales individually, the SDF grid is rebuilt from scratch for each multiplier (there is no simple constant shift that maps one multiplier onto another). The analysis tracks cavity evolution, merging/splitting and disappearance, and produces QSPR-style descriptors.

8. Performance notes

  • Parallel processing of independent cavities / grid regions
  • Spatial sorting + binary search + 3-D culling for atom rejection (typically 85–95 % of candidate atoms discarded)
  • Flat arrays and pooled queues in the flood-fill
  • Cache-friendly vertex merging in Marching Cubes

Typical resolution used interactively is 0.10–0.15 Å. Finer grids increase accuracy at a cubic cost in voxel count.

9. Supported systems

All seven crystal systems and all 230 space groups. The fractional-coordinate grid and the triple-product voxel volume ensure correct results for triclinic, monoclinic, hexagonal and rhombohedral cells.
